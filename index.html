Stop rules for this session

Cut it in half, or skip it, if any of these are true: your approach jump touch is lower than last week's, your forefoot or Achilles is sore on waking, or you slept under six hours. Plyometrics performed tired make you slower — this is the one session where doing it anyway is worse than not doing it.

Technique is the entire return on this session. You've never trained swimming, so drills aren't a warm-up for the real work — they are the work.

The most droppable session in the week. If Whoop recovery has been red two days running, this is the first thing to go — then Sunday's arm block. Never the jump session or the heavy lower work, because those are the goal. Nothing in the 22-month build depends on any single ride.

Fuel

RECOMP AT MAINTENANCE

One target, every day. Separate day-type numbers were more precision than the underlying estimate deserves, and a single figure is far easier to actually hit. Assumes ~184 lb and a desk job.

This sits deliberately between Cronometer's estimate and a full activity-adjusted maintenance figure, because neither is more than a guess until four weeks of weigh-ins test it. Expect maintenance to rise across the block, not fall — the bike goes 45 → 70 min, the runs go 30 → 40 min, and training loads climb. That's another 150–200 kcal of daily demand by week 8.

Rules that govern the numbers

FLOORProtein never moves. 160 g every day, training or not. If a day falls apart, hit protein and let everything else go — it's the single variable protecting muscle while calories stay flat.
LEVERCarbs are the only dial. Move them with the day's demand. Fat has a floor around 55 g for hormone production; never raid it to make room for carbs, and never raid protein for either.
SPACINGFour protein feedings, not two. 160 ÷ 4 is 40 g a feeding, which is about where animal protein clears the leucine threshold that actually triggers the response. Smaller, more frequent hits leave stimulus on the table.
PROXIMITYThe closer to training, the smaller and leaner the meal. Fat and fibre slow gastric emptying, which is what makes you feel heavy under the bar. Far out: eat normally. Close in: carbs and protein only.
FASTEDTwo sessions are never done under-fuelled: Tuesday and Saturday. Central nervous system output is the entire point of both. Everything else — swim, easy run, easy ride, arm block — is fine fasted if that's what fits your morning.
AFTERPost-session carbs scale with what you emptied. Heavy lifting or a long ride earns a real refill. An easy swim or a 30-minute jog doesn't. The protein feeding matters either way; the window is hours wide, so don't sprint home.
DURINGUnder 75 minutes: water and sodium only. Every session in this block qualifies. Past 75 min, 40–60 g carbs per hour.
OVERNIGHTEnd the day on slow protein. Cottage cheese, Greek yoghurt or casein before bed. Overnight is your longest fast and the one place an extra feeding measurably helps.
WATER3.5–4 L on training days, with added sodium on swim days and any session you finish visibly salty.
ADJUSTChange nothing for four weeks. The target is a hypothesis and your weekly average is the test; adjusting sooner means reacting to water weight, and creatine alone will muddy the first fortnight. Weigh first thing, after the bathroom, before food, and read the weekly average only.

Supplements

ITEMDOSERULE
Creatine monohydrate5 gDaily, rest days included, timing genuinely irrelevant. Consistency is the whole mechanism — saturation takes 3–4 weeks with or without a loading phase. Best-evidenced supplement there is, and unusually well matched to repeated maximal jumps.
Vitamin D32,000–4,000 IUTake it with whichever meal has the most fat — it's fat-soluble and absorbs poorly without. Maryland winters make deficiency likely, but get a serum level drawn rather than guessing at a dose.
Magnesium glycinate200–400 mgEvening, well clear of training. Glycinate specifically — citrate and oxide are the forms that send people to the bathroom.
Caffeine200–250 mgSpend it where power matters: Tuesday and Saturday, roughly an hour out. Half-life is 5–6 hours, so count back 8–10 hours from bedtime or you'll watch it show up as wrecked deep sleep on Whoop.
Whey protein25–40 gUse it to close the gap to 160 g on a workday. It's food, not a strategy.
Electrolytes~500 mg sodiumAny session you finish salty, plus every swim.

What to skip, and why

  • AG1 — an expensive multivitamin built on a proprietary blend, so the doses of most ingredients aren't disclosed. Nothing in it beats vegetables plus the three items above. Your instinct here is right.
  • Lustriva — marketed for cognition on thin evidence, none of it training-relevant.
  • BCAAs and EAAs — redundant at 160 g of protein a day. They only help someone under-eating protein.
  • Beta-alanine — genuinely works, but only for efforts of 60 seconds to four minutes. Your sessions are 3-rep sets and zone 2. Revisit when you add threshold running.
  • Pre-workout blends — caffeine at a proprietary-blend markup. Buy caffeine.

Two things that will confuse your scale

Creatine pulls water into muscle — expect 1–3 lb of scale gain in the first fortnight that is not fat and should not trigger a calorie cut. Separately, a hard lower-body day holds water for 24–48 hours afterwards. Both are why the four-week trend rule exists.

4 ANCHORS + 2 FLEX

Four protein anchors of ~40 g, spaced roughly 3–4 hours apart, plus two flexible slots that exist to move carbs toward training. No clock times — the slots float with your day.

Rules

ADHERENCE > PRECISION
ORDERBuild every meal protein-first. Pick the protein, then fill the rest of the plate with carbs to taste. Protein is the macro you'll miss if you leave it to chance; carbs are the one you'll never struggle to hit.
REPEATEat the same 3 breakfasts and 3 lunches. Variety is a tax on adherence, and at 2,740 kcal you're eating a lot of food. Save novelty for dinner, which you're cooking anyway.
CLUSTERCarbs move toward training, fat moves away from it. The meal before a session is rice, bread or fruit with lean protein. The meal furthest from training is where the nuts, oil and egg yolks go.
BULKCook protein twice a week, carbs fresh. Two batches of 1.5 kg chicken or beef on Sunday and Wednesday covers every lunch. Rice and potatoes take 20 minutes and taste far better not reheated — don't batch them.
LIQUIDDrinks are the release valve, not the plan. On a day you're 400 kcal short at 9 p.m., milk and whey close it without a fight. On a normal day, chew your calories — solid food handles fullness better and you have a lot to get through.
SHORTFALLIf you can only fix one macro, fix protein. Missing 40 g of carbs is a rounding error across a week. Missing 40 g of protein repeatedly is the thing that costs you tissue.
OUTRestaurant rule: double the protein, ask for extra rice or bread, skip anything fried. Two orders of grilled meat over one is the single highest-leverage move on any menu — the carbs will take care of themselves.
WEEKENDSaturday and Sunday are 29% of your week. Most people build a solid weekday pattern and then erase the weekly average across two days. You don't need to log them perfectly — you do need them to look roughly like a weekday.
FLOORNever let fat drop below 55 g chasing a carb number, and never let a shortfall come out of protein. Carbs absorb all the variance.

Where this usually breaks

Not at dinner. It breaks in the afternoon, when a session gets pushed and you skip Flex A, then arrive at the evening 700 kcal down and can't physically eat the difference. Keep dates, a whey tub and tuna where you actually are during the day — desk drawer, car, gym bag. Closing a 300 kcal gap at 3 p.m. is trivial; closing a 700 kcal gap at 9 p.m. is not.

Getting the texture right

Freeze 24 h on a level shelf — a tilted pint spins unevenly and no program fixes it. Crumbly after the first spin? Add 1–2 tbsp milk and re-spin; that solves almost everything. Always add a pinch of salt and keep the honey — both lower the freezing point, which is the actual mechanism behind a creamy result. A lean base of protein powder and water freezes into chalk. Mix-ins go in after the spin, on the Mix-In program, or nuts turn to dust. Batch four pints on Sunday prep day so there's always one frozen and ready — the 24-hour lead time is the only thing that makes this fail in practice.

Mobility bites

NO KIT · UNDER 6 MIN EACH

Ankle and hip come before everything else: ankle dorsiflexion limits both jump depth and running form, and hip flexor length is the first thing an engineering chair takes from you.

Bite 1 — Unlock

ON WAKING · 5 MIN

  1. Cat–cow — 10 slow cycles, breathe into the top of the back
  2. 90/90 hip switches — 10 per side, hands off the floor by the last few
  3. World's greatest stretch — 5 per side, hold the rotation two seconds
  4. Deep squat hold — 60 s, heels down, elbows pushing the knees out

Bite 2 — Desk reset

EVERY 2–3 HOURS AT THE DESK · 4 MIN

  1. Doorway pec stretch — 30 s per arm at three heights
  2. Thoracic wall rotation — 8 per side, hips square to the wall
  3. Couch stretch against the chair — 45 s per side, glute squeezed
  4. Chin tucks — 10 slow, hold 3 s each

Bite 3 — Ankle prep

BEFORE TUESDAY, SATURDAY AND EVERY RUN · 5 MIN

  1. Knee-to-wall ankle rocks — 15 per side. Measure it: aim for 5 inches of toe-to-wall clearance with the heel down
  2. Calf raise off a step — 15 slow, full stretch at the bottom
  3. ATG split squat, bodyweight — 8 per side
  4. Hip flexor stretch — 45 s per side
  5. Pogo primers — 2 × 10 tiny bounces, then you're ready to jump

Bite 4 — Decompress

EVENING · 6 MIN

  1. Dead hang — 3 × 30 s. Doubles as pull-up grip and shoulder decompression
  2. 90/90 breathing on the floor — 8 breaths, feet on a wall or couch
  3. Standing hamstring floss — 10 per side
  4. Frog or pigeon — 90 s, whichever your hips ask for

Stop rules

Cut the jump session in half, or skip it, if any of these are true: your approach jump touch is lower than last week's, your forefoot or Achilles is sore on waking, or you slept under six hours. Plyometrics performed tired make you slower.

Retest at week 8: bench 3RM, max strict pull-ups, standing reach touch, and bodyweight under identical conditions each time. If bodyweight has drifted below 176 and the touch has dropped with it, you're eating too little — add food, not sessions.
